MA1508 Calculus · Chapter 4

Applications of Differentiation — rebuilt from the lecture slides

A section-by-section redesign of the Chapter 4 deck: every rule stated with its conditions, every rule proved or given an honest proof sketch, and every section closed with fully worked examples. Source: the Chapter 4 frames of MA1508-lecture note.pdf, expanded against Stewart, Single Variable Calculus, 4th ed., and reworked around data-science applications.

Why this rebuild

The original Chapter 4 frames state each application, quote a result, and move on: the word problems are left with answers but no working, the proofs are absent, and the connection to data science is never made explicit. This deck keeps the lecturer's selection of results and supplies what the slides left implicit.

What is kept

  • The lecturer's section order: rates of change, related rates, maximum and minimum values, shapes of curves, l'Hôpital's rule, optimization, Newton's method, antiderivatives.
  • The Stewart core results — the Extreme Value Theorem, Fermat's theorem, the Closed Interval Method, the first- and second-derivative tests, l'Hôpital's rule, Newton's iteration, the antiderivative table.
  • Every numerical example from the lecture frames, re-derived and checked.

What is added

  • Complete worked solutions to every example, each derivative step named with its rule.
  • Data-science applications alongside the calculus: likelihood-ratio ROC slopes, label shift, M/M/1 queues, least squares and maximum-likelihood as Fermat's theorem, logistic-regression concavity, Rényi entropy, the newsvendor problem, Newton's method as IRLS, PDF-to-CDF and hazard-to-survival antiderivatives.
  • Common-mistakes cards, honest caveats about where the classical word problems do not transfer, and a rules-recap table per section.

The companion to this chapter is the Chapter 3 deck, Differentiation Rules, which supplies the derivative machinery this chapter applies.

The eight sections

4.1Rates of change (bridge) Position, velocity and acceleration on a line and the complete eight-part analysis of (s=t^{3}-6t^{2}+9t+2). Stewart places this material in §3.8; it opens the Chapter 4 deck because it is the first application of differentiation. 4.1′Related rates Differentiating an equation that ties two moving quantities together: the ROC slope as a likelihood ratio, label shift, the M/M/1 queue, the chain rule behind F1 thresholds, and the honest caveat that the ladder problem is not a data-science story. 4.2Maximum and minimum values Fermat's theorem, critical numbers, the Extreme Value Theorem and the Closed Interval Method. The punchline: the normal equations of least squares and maximum-likelihood estimates are Fermat's theorem. Includes the separable-logistic counterexample where no finite maximum exists. 4.3Shapes of curves Increasing/decreasing tests, concavity, inflection points and the second derivative test as the one-dimensional Hessian test. Worked on a cubic, on the log-likelihood of logistic regression, on a test-error curve, on the sigmoid and on entropy. 4.5l'Hôpital's rule The rule and the indeterminate forms it resolves, with the lecture's nine limits. Genuine applications: (lim_{p o0^{+}}pln p=0) makes (0ln 0 := 0) the only continuous value of the cross-entropy loss, and Rényi entropy converges to Shannon entropy. A warning that softmax temperature limits are not l'Hôpital's rule. 4.6Optimization The four classical geometry problems — fencing, can, box, apartments — then elasticity as the revenue-derivative sign, the newsvendor critical-fractile order, and the two-asset minimum-variance portfolio. 4.7Newton's method Newton's iteration (x_{n+1}=x_{n}-f(x_{n})/f'(x_{n})) applied to a log-likelihood: iteratively reweighted least squares. The logistic MLE converges in four steps, (sqrt2) shows quadratic convergence, and the failure modes (bad starts, separable data, saddle points) are named honestly. 4.8Antiderivatives Reversing a derivative: PDF to CDF, hazard to survival, the unidentifiable baseline constant (C), initial-value problems as the way the constant is pinned down, and first-order error propagation.

How to use a section page

Reading order

Each file runs: motivation and notation, then one card per rule or application, then the worked examples, then a common-mistakes card, then a recap table. Cards are numbered, and the footer links move to the previous or next section.

Proof labels

A green Proof block is complete. An amber Sketch block is a proof with one explicitly named gap. Nothing is asserted without a note saying what is missing.

Conventions
  • Every angle is in radians unless a degree sign is shown.
  • Worked examples show every step; each differentiation names the rule used.
  • Where a classical Stewart problem is included for completeness, the page says plainly whether it has an honest data-science reading — some are pure calculus training.
