The three graphs, stacked

The vertical line is the current time. Velocity is the slope of the position graph; acceleration is the slope of the velocity graph and the concavity of the position graph.

When is it speeding up?

The particle speeds up when velocity and acceleration have the same sign, and slows down when they have opposite signs. The sign of the acceleration alone does not decide this.

IntervalSign of vSign of aSame sign?Motion

At \(t=2\) the acceleration is momentarily zero, so the particle is neither speeding up nor slowing down at that instant. At \(t=1\) and \(t=3\) the velocity is zero and the particle is momentarily at rest.

The lecture's eight parts, answered
  1. (a) \(v(t)=3t^{2}-12t+9=3(t-1)(t-3)\).
  2. (b) \(v(2)=-3\) m/s and \(v(4)=9\) m/s.
  3. (c) At rest when \(v=0\), that is \(t=1\) and \(t=3\).
  4. (d) Moving forward when \(v>0\): on \([0,1)\) and \((3,\infty)\). Moving backward when \(v<0\): on \((1,3)\).
  5. (e) The particle starts at \(s=2\), moves forward to \(s=6\) at \(t=1\), reverses to \(s=2\) at \(t=3\), then moves forward to \(s=22\) at \(t=5\).
  6. (f) Total distance on \([0,5]\) is 28 m, while the net displacement is \(22-2=20\) m.
  7. (g) \(a(t)=6t-12\), so \(a(4)=12\) m/s².
  8. (h) Speeding up on \(1<t<2\) and \(t>3\); slowing down on \(0\le t<1\) and \(2<t<3\).

Distance is not displacement

Because the particle turns around, the total distance travelled exceeds the net displacement. Distance must be accumulated leg by leg, splitting the interval at every instant where the velocity is zero.
