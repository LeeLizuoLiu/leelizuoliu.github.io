MA1508 Calculus · Chapter 3

Differentiation Rules — rebuilt from the lecture slides

A section-by-section redesign of the Chapter 3 deck: every rule stated with its conditions, every rule proved or given an honest proof sketch, and every section closed with fully worked examples. Source: the Chapter 3 frames of MA1508-lecture note.pdf, expanded against Stewart, Single Variable Calculus, 4th ed.

Why this rebuild

The original Chapter 3 frames are animated builds: the same frame is repeated with one extra bullet each time (Section 3.1 alone occupies six near-identical pages), rules appear without justification, and most examples stop at the answer. Useful for a live lecture, but hard to revise from.

This deck keeps the lecturer's own selection of rules, examples and warnings, and supplies what the animation left implicit:

What is kept

  • The exact rule list of each section, in the lecture's order.
  • The lecturer's examples, including the counterexample \((fg)' \neq f'g'\) with \(f=x^{3}\), \(g=x^{6}\).
  • The warning about radian measure, and the reciprocal and root rules.

What is added

  • Every rule proved from the limit definition, or marked as a proof sketch with the omitted detail named.
  • Two to three complete worked examples per section, every algebraic step shown.
  • Common-mistakes cards and a rules-recap table per section.

The five sections

3.1Derivatives of polynomials and exponential functions Constant, power, constant-multiple, sum and difference rules, the negative-power and root extensions, and \((e^{x})' = e^{x}\), \((a^{x})' = a^{x}\ln a\) — each proved from the difference quotient. Includes the horizontal-tangent example and the term-by-term polynomial assembly. 3.2The product and quotient rules The product rule proved by the add-and-subtract trick; the quotient rule derived from it and then proved independently from the limit definition; the reciprocal rule; and the lecturer's counterexample showing \((fg)' \neq f'g'\). 3.3Derivatives of trigonometric functions Why radians are mandatory; the two fundamental limits \(\lim_{t\to0}\sin t/t = 1\) and \(\lim_{t\to0}(\cos t - 1)/t = 0\) proved by the squeeze theorem; then \((\sin x)' = \cos x\), \((\cos x)' = -\sin x\) and the four remaining derivatives. 3.4The chain rule The rule in function and Leibniz form, with the rate argument and a rigorous proof sketch that names the \(\Delta u = 0\) hole; then power, exponential and trigonometric composites, and problems combining the chain rule with the product and quotient rules. 3.5Implicit differentiation Explicit versus implicit descriptions; why every \(y\)-term drags a \(dy/dx\) behind it; the slope and tangent line on \(x^{2}+y^{2}=25\) and \(x^{3}+y^{3}=6xy\); and the second derivative computed implicitly.

How to use a section page

Reading order

Each file runs: motivation and notation, then one card per rule with its proof, then the worked examples, then a common-mistakes card, then a recap table. Cards are numbered, and the footer links move to the previous or next section.

Proof labels

A green Proof block is complete. An amber Sketch block is a proof with one explicitly named gap — used only for the two long arguments (the chain rule and the implicit-function formula). Nothing is asserted without a note saying what is missing.

Conventions
  • Every angle is in radians unless a degree sign is shown.
  • Proofs use the limit definition \(f'(x) = \lim_{h\to0}\dfrac{f(x+h)-f(x)}{h}\) and the limit laws.
  • Forward references are flagged, so a rule is never applied before it has been proved in this chapter.
