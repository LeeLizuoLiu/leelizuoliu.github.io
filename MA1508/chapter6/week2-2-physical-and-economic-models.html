Physics · model → contribution

1. Hydrostatic force

Water pushes on a submerged plate. Gauge pressureGauge pressurePressure measured above atmospheric. It grows with depth and does not depend on the shape of the container.\(p=\rho gh\) grows with depth, \(p=\rho gh\), so the plate cannot be handled as one product \(pA\): deeper strips are pushed harder.

Cut the plate into horizontal strips. A strip at depth \(h\) has area \(w(h)\,dh\) and feels

\[dF=p\,dA=\rho gh\,w(h)\,dh,\qquad F=\int\rho gh\,w(h)\,dh.\]

For water in Example 1, use \(\rho=1000\) kg/m³ and \(g=9.8\) m/s².

Example 1

A vertical rectangular plate is 2 m wide and extends from the water surface to depth 3 m. Find the force on one side from the water pressure above atmospheric pressure.

Show answerHide answer
  1. Use \(w(h)=2\), \(0\le h\le3\). Pressure varies with depth; it is not uniform.
  2. \(F=\int_0^3(1000)(9.8)h(2)dh=19600(9/2)=88200\) N.
\(88200\) N.
Physics · mass and moment

2. A rod balances at its centre of mass

Lay a rod on a fulcrum and it tips toward the denser end. A rod of varying linear densityLinear densityMass per unit length, in kg/m. A short piece of length \(dx\) then has mass \(\rho(x)\,dx\).\(\rho(x)=\dfrac{dm}{dx}\) balances past its midpoint, toward the denser side.

Cut the rod into short pieces. A piece at \(x\) has mass \(\rho(x)\,dx\) and contributes \(x\,dm\) to the momentMomentMass times its distance from a point or axis. It measures how strongly that mass would turn the rod about the point.\(M=\int x\,dm\) about 0.

Adding the pieces gives the moment of the whole rod, its total tendency to turn about 0. The centre of mass is the point where a single particle of the total mass would have the same moment:

\[dm=\rho(x)\,dx,\quad dM=x\,dm,\quad m=\int_a^b\rho(x)dx,\quad \bar x=\frac{M}{m}=\frac{\int_a^b x\rho(x)dx}{\int_a^b\rho(x)dx}\quad(m>0).\]

Example 2

A rod on \([0,1]\) m has density \(\rho(x)=1+x\) kg/m, with \(x\) expressed numerically in metres. Find its mass and centre of mass.

Show answerHide answer
  1. \(m=\int_0^1(1+x)dx=3/2\) kg.
  2. \(M=\int_0^1(x+x^2)dx=5/6\) kg·m.
  3. \(\bar x=(5/6)/(3/2)=5/9\) m, shifted toward the denser end.
Mass \(1.5\) kg; centre \(5/9\) m.

Example 3

For a rod of positive constant density on \([a,b]\), show that its centre of mass is the midpoint.

Show answerHide answer
  1. Mass is \(\rho(b-a)\). Moment is \(\rho(b^2-a^2)/2\).
  2. Their ratio is \((a+b)/2\).
\(\bar x=(a+b)/2\).
Geometry · centroid

3. A uniform lamina

A lamina is a flat plate, a region of the plane with no thickness. With the same mass per unit area everywhere, its balance point depends only on the shape; that point is called the centroidCentroidThe point where a flat plate of uniform density balances — the average position of its area.. A lopsided region balances off-centre.

Cut the lamina into vertical strips. A strip at \(x\) has area \(dB=(f-g)\,dx\) and centre \(\left(x,\tfrac{f+g}{2}\right)\), so its moments about the \(y\)-axis and the \(x\)-axis are

\[dM_y=x\,dB=x(f-g)\,dx,\qquad dM_x=\tfrac{f+g}{2}\,dB=\tfrac12(f+g)(f-g)\,dx.\]

Summing the strips, with \((f+g)(f-g)=f^2-g^2\):

\[M_y=\int_a^b x(f-g)\,dx,\qquad M_x=\tfrac12\int_a^b(f^2-g^2)\,dx,\qquad B=\int_a^b(f-g)\,dx.\]

Uniform density multiplies all three by the same \(\rho\), so it cancels when each moment is divided by the area:

\[\bar x=\frac1B\int_a^b x(f-g)\,dx,\qquad \bar y=\frac1{2B}\int_a^b(f^2-g^2)\,dx\quad(B>0).\]

Example 4

Find the centroid of the triangle under \(y=x\), above the axis, on \([0,1]\).

Show answerHide answer
  1. \(B=\int_0^1x dx=1/2\).
  2. \(\bar x=(\int_0^1x^2dx)/(1/2)=2/3\); \(\bar y=(\tfrac12\int_0^1x^2dx)/(1/2)=1/3\).
Centroid \((2/3,1/3)\).
Economics · accumulation

4. Cost and consumer surplus

Two questions from a running business. A factory pays 100 dollars to set up a run and \(2+0.1q\) dollars for each unit: what does a run of 10 units cost? At a market price of 10 dollars with demand \(D(q)=20-q\), how much do buyers gain?

Both totals add up many small contributions, so both are integrals.

Cost. Marginal cost \(C'(q)\) is the cost of one more unit, so a thin batch of \(dq\) units costs \(C'(q)\,dq\). Adding the batches and the fixed cost \(C(0)\):

\[C(Q)=C(0)+\int_0^Q C'(q)\,dq.\]

Example 5

Let \(C(0)=100\) dollars and \(C\prime(q)=2+0.1q\) dollars per unit. Find \(C(10)\).

Show answerHide answer
  1. Variable cost is \(\int_0^{10}(2+0.1q)dq=[2q+0.05q^2]_0^{10}=25\) dollars.
  2. Add the fixed cost: \(C(10)=100+25=125\).
\(125\) dollars.

Consumer surplus (CS). A unit at quantity \(q\) is worth \(D(q)\) to buyers but costs \(P\), so it adds \(D(q)-P\) to their consumer surplusConsumer surplusWhat buyers gain: total willingness to pay minus what they actually pay.\(\mathrm{CS}=\int_0^Q[D(q)-P]\,dq\). Adding up to the sales level \(Q\):

\[d(\mathrm{CS})=[D(q)-P]dq,\qquad \mathrm{CS}=\int_0^Q[D(q)-P]dq.\]

Example 6

For \(D(q)=20-q\) dollars per unit and price \(P=10\), find the sales level and consumer surplus.

Show answerHide answer
  1. Solve \(20-Q=10\), giving \(Q=10\).
  2. \(\mathrm{CS}=\int_0^{10}(10-q)dq=[10q-q^2/2]_0^{10}=50\) dollars.
Sales level 10 units; consumer surplus 50 dollars.
Biology · flow

5. Blood flow through a vessel

How much blood passes a cross-section each second? In laminar flowLaminar flowSmooth flow in parallel layers that slide past each other. The speed depends only on distance from the wall, so it is largest at the centre.\(v=v(r)\), blood moves fastest at the centre of the vessel and slowest at the wall, so the speed varies across the section.

Cut the cross-section into thin rings. A ring at radius \(r\) has area about \(2\pi r\,dr\) and speed \(v(r)\), so it carries

\[dQ\approx v(r)\,2\pi r\,dr,\qquad Q=\int_0^R2\pi r\,v(r)dr.\]

Example 7

If laminar flow has profile \(v(r)=K(R^2-r^2)\), with \(K>0\), find the flow rate.

Show answerHide answer
  1. \(Q=2\pi K\int_0^R(R^2r-r^3)dr\).
  2. \(Q=2\pi K(R^4/2-R^4/4)=\pi K R^4/2\).
  3. For Poiseuille’s profile, \(K=\Delta P/(4\eta\ell)\), giving \(Q=\pi\Delta P R^4/(8\eta\ell)\). This formula uses a steady laminar-flow model.
\(Q=\pi K R^4/2\).
Biology · ink dilution

6. Concentration at a sensor

Flow through a vessel can be measured from outside. Imagine the vessel is injected with a known mass of ink, and you watch the concentration \(c(t)\) downstream.

In a short time \(dt\) a volume \(Q\,dt\) passes the sensor, carrying ink \(dm=Qc(t)\,dt\). If all the ink is recovered,

\[m=Q\int_0^T c(t)dt,\qquad Q=\frac{m}{\int_0^Tc(t)dt}.\]

Example 8

A 6 mg injection produces an integrated concentration of 30 mg·s/L. Find the model flow rate.

Show answerHide answer
  1. \(Q=6/30=0.2\) L/s.
  2. Multiply by 60 seconds per minute to obtain 12 L/min.
\(0.2\) L/s, or 12 L/min.
Biology · rate to total

7. Total change from a rate

A quantity changes at a known rate at every moment. How much does it change over a stretch of time?

A rate \(n'(t)\) adds \(n'(t)\,dt\) over a short time \(dt\). Adding these contributions gives the net change:

\[n(t_2)-n(t_1)=\int_{t_1}^{t_2}n'(t)\,dt.\]

Example 9

A population grows at rate \(n\prime(t)=4e^{0.2t}\) individuals/week. Find its increase over the first 5 weeks.

Show answerHide answer
  1. \(n(5)-n(0)=\int_0^54e^{0.2t}dt=[20e^{0.2t}]_0^5\).
  2. The result gives the increase; add \(n(0)\) if the final population is needed.
\(20(e-1)\) individuals under the continuous model.
