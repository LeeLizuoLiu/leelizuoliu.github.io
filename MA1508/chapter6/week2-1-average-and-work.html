Average Value and Work

From equal-area averages to lifting a thin slice of water.

  • Average value
  • Work and pumping

Stewart, Single Variable Calculus: Concepts and Contexts, 4th ed.: §6.5, printed p. 460; §6.6, printed p. 464.

Definition → derivation

1. What constant value gives the same total?

For continuous \(f\) on \([a,b]\), with \(a<b\), take one sample in each of \(n\) equal intervals: \(\Delta x=(b-a)/n\). The arithmetic mean tends to

\[\frac1n\sum_{i=1}^n f(x_i^*)=\frac{1}{b-a}\sum_{i=1}^n f(x_i^*)\Delta x\;\longrightarrow\; f_{\rm avg}=\frac{1}{b-a}\int_a^bf(x)\,dx.\]

Average and \(f\) have the same units. For \(f\ge0\), the average is the height of an equal-area rectangle.

A data feed delivers records at rate \(r(t)=6t\) thousand per hour during the hour \(0\le t\le1\). The constant rate that delivers the same total is the height at which the two shaded regions have equal area. That height is the average arrival rate over the hour.

Here \(r_{\rm avg}=3\) thousand/hour. For signed \(f\), the same formula uses signed area.

Theorem

2. Is the average attained?

Mean Value Theorem for Integrals. If \(f\) is continuous on \([a,b]\), where \(a<b\), then some \(c\in[a,b]\) satisfies

\[\int_a^b f(x)\,dx=f(c)(b-a),\quad\text{so }f(c)=f_{\rm avg}.\]
Proof

Continuity gives a minimum \(m\) and maximum \(M\). Thus \(m(b-a)\le\int_a^bf\le M(b-a)\), so \(m\le f_{\rm avg}\le M\). The Intermediate Value TheoremTheorem · Intermediate Value TheoremIf \(f\) is continuous on the closed interval \([a,b]\), then for every \(N\) strictly between \(f(a)\) and \(f(b)\) there is a \(c\in(a,b)\) with \(f(c)=N\).\(N\) between \(f(a)\) and \(f(b)\;\Longrightarrow\;f(c)=N\)A continuous curve cannot jump over a level. gives a \(c\) with \(f(c)=f_{\rm avg}\).

Example 1

Find the average of \(f(x)=x^2\) on \([0,2]\), and a point attaining it.

Show answerHide answer
  1. \(f_{\rm avg}=\tfrac12[x^3/3]_0^2=4/3\).
  2. Solve \(c^2=4/3\). Only the positive solution lies in \([0,2]\): \(c=2/\sqrt3\).
Average \(4/3\), attained at \(c=2/\sqrt3\).

Example 2

Find the average of \(f(x)=\sin x\) on \([0,\pi]\), and points where it is attained.

Show answerHide answer
  1. The integral is \([-\cos x]_0^\pi=2\); divide by interval length \(\pi\).
  2. The equation \(\sin c=2/\pi\) has two solutions in \([0,\pi]\).
Average \(2/\pi\); \(c=\arcsin(2/\pi)\) or \(\pi-\arcsin(2/\pi)\).

3. Work by a changing force

Work is force times distance. A constant force \(F\) acting through a distance \(d\) in its own direction does the work

\[W=Fd\qquad(\mathrm N\cdot\mathrm m=\mathrm J).\]

Holding a book still is no work, because nothing moves.

Lifting a \(1.2\) kg book \(0.7\) m takes a force equal to its weight, \(1.2(9.8)\approx11.8\) N, so \(W\approx11.8(0.7)\approx8.2\) J.

A spring is different: according to Hooke’s lawLaw · Hooke’s LawA stretched spring pulls back with a force proportional to its stretch; \(k\) is the spring constant, in N/m.\(F(x)=kx\)Twice the stretch, twice the force., its force grows as it stretches, so \(W=Fd\) no longer applies. Cut the motion into thin pieces, short enough that the force is nearly constant on each. The work on one piece is then

\[\Delta W\approx F(x)\,\Delta x\qquad(F(x)\ \text{along the motion}).\]

Adding the pieces and letting their number grow gives the exact work for a continuous force:

\[W=\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{i=1}^{n}F(x_i^*)\Delta x=\int_a^bF(x)\,dx.\]

Example 3

A spring has constant \(k=200\) N/m. Find the work to stretch it from 0 to 0.1 m beyond its natural length.

Show answerHide answer
  1. Hooke’s law gives the required force \(F(x)=kx=200x\) N.
  2. \(W=\int_0^{0.1}200x dx=[100x^2]_0^{0.1}=1\) J.
\(1\) J.
Model → contribution → integral

4. Pump water one layer at a time

A tank of water has to be emptied through an outlet at height \(H\). Water at height \(y\) must be lifted \(H-y\), and each layer is lifted a different distance, so the total is not a single product \(Fd\).

Choose height \(y\) from the bottom. With constant density \(\rho\) and cross-sectional area \(A(y)\),

\[dV=A(y)\,dy\quad\longrightarrow\quad dm=\rho A(y)\,dy\quad\longrightarrow\quad dW=\underbrace{\rho gA(y)\,dy}_{\text{weight}}\underbrace{(H-y)}_{\text{lift}}.\]

Example 4

A tank has horizontal cross-sectional area 2 m² and contains water to a depth of 3 m. Find the work to pump all the water to an outlet 4 m above the bottom. Use density \(\rho=1000\) kg/m³ and \(g=9.8\) m/s².

Show answerHide answer
  1. At height \(y\in[0,3]\), a slice has volume \(2dy\), mass \(2000dy\), and weight \(19600dy\) N.
  2. The lift distance is \(4-y\), so \(W=19600\int_0^3(4-y)dy\).
  3. The integral is \([4y-y^2/2]_0^3=7.5\). Thus \(W=147000\) J, assuming ideal pumping.
\(147000\) J.
Application

5. A conical tank

Example 5

A conical tank has height \(H>0\), rim radius \(R>0\), and water depth \(0\le h\le H\). Find the ideal work to pump all the water to the rim. Assume constant density \(\rho\) and gravitational acceleration \(g\).

Show answerHide answer
  1. At height \(y\), \(r(y)=Ry/H\), so \(A(y)=\pi R^2y^2/H^2\). The layer rises \(H-y\).
  2. Its volume is \(A(y)dy\), mass \(\rho A(y)dy\), and weight \(\rho g A(y)dy\). Therefore \(dW=\rho g A(y)(H-y)dy\).
  3. Integrate from tip to water surface: \(\displaystyle W=\frac{\rho g\pi R^2}{H^2}\int_0^h y^2(H-y)\,dy\).
\(\displaystyle W=\frac{\rho g\pi R^2}{H^2}\left(\frac{Hh^3}{3}-\frac{h^4}{4}\right)\) joules when SI units are used.
